Read the gdpr.md source doc in the spikes/eu-regulations folder and extract Roles, Requirements, Obligations, and Capabilities in full, and store them in JSON format in a gdpr.json file in the spikes/graph-ingestion3 folder. Scope: core obligation-bearing provisions only — Article 4 (definitions, role-sourcing only: Controller, Processor, joint controller, representative, Data Protection Officer — not a Requirement source in its own right, same relationship CRA's Art. 3 and NIS2's Art. 3 had to their regulations' duty articles), Chapter II (Art. 5-11: principles relating to processing, lawfulness of processing, conditions for consent (including child's consent), special-category and criminal-conviction data, processing not requiring identification), Chapter III (Art. 12-22: data-subject rights, extracted as the Controller/Processor duty to fulfil them — transparent information and modalities, information/access, rectification and erasure, notification duty on rectification/erasure/restriction, data portability, right to object, automated individual decision-making) plus Art. 23 only where it imposes a direct duty rather than granting Member States a restriction option, and Chapter IV Section 1 (Art. 24-39: responsibility of the controller, data protection by design and by default, joint controllers, representatives, processor obligations and the processor/sub-processor contract chain, processing under the authority of the controller/processor, records of processing activities, cooperation with the supervisory authority, security of processing, breach notification to the authority and to the data subject, data protection impact assessment and prior consultation, and DPO designation/position/tasks). Also include Chapter V (Art. 44-49: international transfers — general principle, transfers by adequacy decision, transfers subject to appropriate safeguards, binding corporate rules, derogations for specific situations) since these impose direct duties on the Controller/Processor, not just Commission/Member-State mechanics.

Exclude purely institutional/procedural material: Chapter I's scope-setting articles (Art. 1-3, aside from Art. 4's role-sourcing role), Chapter IV Section 2 (Art. 40-43: codes of conduct and certification — voluntary compliance paths, not duties, the same "may" exclusion CRA's methodology applied to its Art. 13(10)-(11) public-archive option), Chapter VI (independent supervisory authorities), Chapter VII (cooperation and consistency / EDPB mechanics), Chapter VIII (Art. 77-84: remedies, liability and administrative fines — enforcement consequences, not operational duties, the same reasoning CRA's methodology used to exclude penalties), Chapter IX (Art. 85-91: Member State derogations for specific processing situations — permissive options for national legislatures, not entity duties, the same treatment NIS2 gave Member State transposition provisions), and Chapters X-XI (delegated acts, committee procedure, final provisions).

Role guidance: mint Controller, Processor, joint controller (Art. 26 imposes a distinct arrangement duty beyond a single Controller's, so treat it as its own Role rather than folding it into Controller — the same call CRA made minting "Substantial modifier" outside the Art. 3 glossary), representative (Art. 27), and Data Protection Officer (Art. 38-39 impose duties directly on the DPO — cooperating with the supervisory authority, secrecy, task performance — distinct from the Controller/Processor's duty to designate one under Art. 37, so it's a Role in its own right, not just an Obligation on Controller). Do not mint Recipient or Third party (Art. 4(9)-(10)) as Roles — they're defined terms the text uses to describe data flows, not actors the Regulation assigns duties to. Do not mint Data subject as a Role bearing Obligations — data subjects hold rights, not duties; a right in Chapter III becomes an Obligation on whichever of Controller/Processor must fulfil it, the same "extract the duty that actually lands on a duty-bearing actor" principle NIS2 applied when Member-State-phrased duties actually landed on essential/important entities.

Granularity: extract ALL requirements and ALL obligations — do not cluster multiple distinct duties from different paragraphs or lettered points into a single Requirement/Obligation pair just because they share an article. Work at the paragraph (or lettered/numbered sub-point) level: each numbered paragraph or lettered point that imposes a distinct duty gets its own Requirement node and its own Obligation node. This matters most in Art. 5(1) (six lettered data-processing principles, (a)-(f), each its own capacity to satisfy), Art. 28(3) (the processor-contract content list, (a)-(h)), Art. 32(1) (the four lettered security measures, (a)-(d)), and Art. 35(7) (the four lettered DPIA content requirements) — treat these the way CRA's Annex I Part I lettered points and NIS2's Art. 21(2) measures were each split into their own Requirement. Merge two paragraphs into one Requirement only where one is a direct cross-reference restating a duty the other already imposes, and note the merge decision in the `_comment` field.

Convergence: check the existing Capability list from cra.json and nis2.json before minting a new one — several GDPR duties (security of processing under Art. 32, breach notification under Art. 33-34, records/documentation under Art. 30, access control implicit in Art. 32(1)(b)) are the same underlying capacity CRA's Annex I and NIS2's Art. 21(2) already named (e.g. `Security Logging`, `Access Control & Authentication`, `Incident Handling`). It does not justify merging the Obligations themselves: Obligation is 1:1 with its Role, so where the same duty text recurs for both Controller and Processor (e.g. Art. 32's security-of-processing duty, which is addressed to both), mint two distinct Obligation nodes with role-specific suffixes ("...as Controller" / "...as Processor") wherever the underlying text would otherwise be identical — the same fix CRA's methodology required for Manufacturer/Importer/Distributor collisions and NIS2's for Essential/Important entity collisions.
