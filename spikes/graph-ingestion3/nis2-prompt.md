Scope: core obligation-bearing provisions only — Article 3(1)-(2) (essential/important entity classification) and Article 3(3)-(4) (the entity registration-information duty), Article 20 (management-body governance duty), Article 21 (cybersecurity risk-management measures, including the ten lettered measures in Article 21(2)(a)-(j)), and Article 23 (reporting obligations, including the notification-timeline sub-duties in Article 23(4)(a)-(e)). Exclude purely institutional/procedural articles — Member State cybersecurity strategy and authority establishment (Art. 7-11), the Cooperation Group/CSIRTs network/EU-CyCLONe (Art. 14-17), ENISA reporting and peer review (Art. 18-19), coordinated vulnerability disclosure infrastructure (Art. 12), jurisdiction and the ENISA/domain-name registries (Art. 26-28), supervision and enforcement powers, penalties, and committee/delegated-act procedure (Art. 31 onward) — unless they impose a direct duty on an essential or important entity. Also exclude Article 29-30 (voluntary information sharing/notification — permissive, not mandatory) and Annex I/II (these classify which entities count as essential/important by sector; they're the sourcing context for Role, not a Requirement source in their own right — same relationship CRA's Art. 3 definitions had to CRA's later duty articles).

Most of this Directive's duties are phrased indirectly — "Member States shall ensure that essential and important entities [do X]" — rather than CRA's direct "Manufacturers shall [do X]." Extract the substantive duty that lands on the entity, not the Member-State transposition obligation wrapping it: the Requirement/Obligation pair is about what the essential/important entity must do, not what the Member State must ensure. Do not mint "Member State" as a Role.

Granularity: extract ALL requirements and ALL obligations — do not cluster multiple distinct duties from different paragraphs or lettered points into a single Requirement/Obligation pair just because they share an article. Work at the paragraph (or lettered sub-point) level: each numbered paragraph or lettered point that imposes a distinct duty gets its own Requirement node and its own Obligation node. This matters most in Article 21(2) (ten lettered risk-management measures — each is its own Requirement, the way CRA's Annex I Part I lettered points were) and Article 23(4) (the early-warning/incident-notification/intermediate-report/final-report timeline — each deadline is its own Requirement, the way CRA's Art. 14(3) notification stages were). Merge two paragraphs into one Requirement only in the narrow case where one is a direct cross-reference restating a duty the other already imposes — and note the merge decision in the _comment field.

It's expected and desired for multiple Obligations, across different Roles, to REQUIRE the same Capability where they draw on the same underlying capacity — that cross-role convergence is the point of the model, and it's also the point of this second regulation: check the existing Capability list from cra.json before minting a new one, since several of NIS2's Article 21(2) measures (incident handling, vulnerability handling, supply chain security, access control) are the same underlying capacity CRA's Annex I already named. It does not justify merging the Obligations themselves: Obligation is 1:1 with its Role, so the same duty text recurring for both Essential entity and Important entity must be minted as two distinct Obligation nodes, not one shared across HAS edges — since Obligation identity is a hash of its text, give each a role-specific suffix ("...as Essential entity" / "...as Important entity") wherever the underlying text would otherwise be identical, the same fix CRA's methodology required for Manufacturer/Importer/Distributor collisions.
